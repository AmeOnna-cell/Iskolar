# **Scholarship Awareness Survey: Synthesis of Findings, Concerns and Issues, and Proposed System**

Based on the survey responses, the proposed system should address more than simply providing a list of scholarships. The main problem identified is the difficulty students experience in discovering, understanding, determining their eligibility for, and applying to scholarship opportunities in a timely manner.

The findings suggest developing a Centralized Scholarship Information and Management System that allows students to discover relevant scholarships, check their qualifications, understand application procedures, and receive timely notifications about deadlines.

## **1\. Synthesis of Findings**

The survey titled Scholarship Awareness and Information Needs Among College Students gathered 29 responses, of which 27 respondents agreed to participate in the study. The respondents came from different academic programs and year levels, providing insights into how college students discover scholarship opportunities and the difficulties they encounter when accessing scholarship information.

The findings reveal that students generally have some awareness of scholarship programs, particularly commonly discussed opportunities such as the Tertiary Education Subsidy (TES), Tulong Dunong Program (TDP), DOST-SEI, and OWWA scholarships. However, awareness of available opportunities does not necessarily translate into a clear understanding of the different scholarship programs, their qualifications, and their application procedures.

One recurring concern is the difficulty of finding centralized, updated, and reliable scholarship information. Respondents commonly rely on Facebook pages, school announcements, acquaintances, and official websites to discover scholarship opportunities. Although these sources provide useful information, scholarship announcements are often scattered across different platforms. Some respondents also mentioned that information from online announcements and word-of-mouth sources may differ, making it difficult to determine which information is accurate and current.

Another significant finding concerns the complexity of scholarship requirements and eligibility criteria. Many respondents identified eligibility requirements as the most important information they need before deciding to apply. These include academic qualifications, general weighted averages (GWA), financial background, geographical qualifications, and program-specific requirements. Some students expressed difficulty determining whether they qualify for a scholarship, while others reported spending time preparing documents for opportunities they were ultimately ineligible for.

The survey also highlights the problem of late announcements and missed application deadlines. Several respondents shared experiences of discovering scholarship opportunities too late, receiving announcements on the final day of application, or being unable to secure the necessary documents before the deadline. These experiences indicate that the availability of scholarship information alone is insufficient if students are not informed early enough to prepare their applications.

Furthermore, respondents expressed the need for simpler explanations and step-by-step application instructions. Scholarship procedures can involve multiple documents, offices, qualifications, and submission periods. Without clear guidance, students may find the process confusing and discouraging.

When asked about their preferred features for a scholarship information website, respondents frequently identified search and filtering options, eligibility checkers, deadline reminders, and updated scholarship announcements. Email notifications were particularly preferred as a means of keeping students informed, although some also suggested SMS and website notifications.

Overall, the findings indicate that students need a more accessible, organized, reliable, and student-centered approach to scholarship information. A centralized system could help bridge the gap between scholarship opportunities and the students who need them by making relevant information easier to discover, understand, and act upon.

### **Key implications for the proposed system**

The survey suggests five major areas that should guide system development:

1\. Accessibility and Centralization

The system should serve as a single access point for scholarship opportunities. Rather than requiring students to browse multiple Facebook pages, websites, and announcements, they should be able to find relevant information in one location.

2\. Personalization and Eligibility Matching

The system should not simply display every available scholarship. It should help students identify opportunities that match their academic, financial, and personal qualifications. This reduces unnecessary applications and helps students focus on scholarships they can realistically pursue.

3\. Clarity and User Guidance

Scholarship information should be presented in a way that students can easily understand. Technical terms, complicated requirements, and application procedures should be organized into understandable sections.

4\. Timely Information and Notifications

The system should help prevent missed opportunities through updated announcements, deadline tracking, and notification mechanisms. Information must also distinguish between open, upcoming, closed, and potentially unverified opportunities.

5\. Application Preparation and Monitoring

Students should be able to monitor their progress in preparing scholarship applications. A document checklist and application status tracker could help them organize requirements and keep track of their applications.

## **3\. Applying Design Thinking and Human-Centered Design**

To determine what kind of system should be developed, the survey findings can be examined using Design Thinking and Human-Centered Design (HCD).

These approaches emphasize understanding the needs, experiences, and difficulties of the intended users before deciding on a technological solution.

Instead of immediately developing a website simply because students requested one, the development process should first identify the actual problems students experience when searching for and applying to scholarships.

### **A. Design Thinking Approach**

Design Thinking commonly follows five stages:

1. Empathize – Understand the students  
   Identify the difficulties students experience when looking for scholarship information.  
   Survey evidence: Students struggle with scattered announcements, unclear eligibility requirements, unreliable information, and late notifications.  
2. Define – Identify the actual problem  
   Formulate the central problem based on the students' experiences.  
   Problem statement: College students lack a centralized and reliable platform that allows them to discover relevant scholarships, understand their qualifications, and prepare applications before deadlines.  
3. Ideate – Develop possible solutions  
   Identify system features that directly address the problems.  
   Possible solutions: Scholarship database, eligibility checker, search filters, application guides, document checklist, and deadline notifications.  
4. Prototype – Create an initial system design  
   Develop wireframes and interactive prototypes of the proposed system.  
   Possible prototypes: Student dashboard, scholarship listings, scholarship details, eligibility assessment, and notification interface.  
5. Test – Evaluate with actual students  
   Allow students to interact with the prototype and evaluate whether the features address their needs.  
   Possible evaluation: Determine whether students can easily find scholarships, understand their requirements, identify their eligibility, and track deadlines.

### **B. Human-Centered Design Principles**

Human-Centered Design places the intended users at the center of the system development process.

For the proposed scholarship system, this means:

* User needs first: Features should be based on the actual difficulties expressed by students rather than assumptions made by developers.  
* Simplicity: Scholarship information should be easy to understand, navigate, and access.  
* Accessibility: The system should accommodate students with different levels of technical knowledge and different academic backgrounds.  
* User control: Students should be able to manage their preferences, choose notification methods, and track their own applications.  
* Continuous improvement: Feedback from students should be collected and used to improve the system.

For example, instead of displaying a lengthy scholarship announcement copied directly from a government website, the system could present the information using organized sections such as eligibility, benefits, requirements, deadlines, and application procedures.

This makes the information more understandable without removing the original source or official details.

## **6\. Final Synthesis: From Survey Findings to System Proposal**

The survey establishes that the primary challenge experienced by college students is not necessarily the complete absence of scholarship opportunities, but rather the difficulty of accessing and understanding information about them. Applying Design Thinking and Human-Centered Design, the proposed system should focus on addressing these actual student experiences rather than simply digitizing existing scholarship announcements.

## **7\. Additional Recommendation: How to Make the System More Human-Centered**

One important consideration is that a centralized website alone does not guarantee that students will receive accurate or timely scholarship information.

I recommend incorporating these three considerations into the actual system design:

1. Verified information: Every scholarship listing should identify its official source, date last updated, and verification status. Administrators should be responsible for maintaining accurate information.  
2. Explainable eligibility checker: Instead of simply displaying "Eligible" or "Not Eligible," the system should explain why a student matches or does not match the requirements. It should also distinguish between confirmed eligibility and potential eligibility when some information is missing.  
3. Mobile-friendly interface: Students should be able to access scholarship information through their phones, particularly because social media and mobile browsing are common ways of discovering announcements.

My overall recommendation: Make the eligibility checker the system's central distinguishing feature, supported by a centralized scholarship database and notification system. This makes the proposed platform more than an announcement website: it becomes a tool that actively helps students identify, understand, and prepare for scholarship opportunities.

Research note: Your survey has 27 consenting respondents, so present these findings as insights from your respondents rather than conclusions representing all college students. Also, the eligibility checker should initially serve as a preliminary assessment tool, not an official determination of scholarship approval.
